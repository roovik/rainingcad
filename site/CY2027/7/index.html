Devon Rex

The pixie of the cat world. A big-eared imp in a suit of soft waves.

The Devon Rex looks like it wandered in from a fairy tale. It turned up in Devon, England, in 1960, when a curly-coated kitten named Kirlee was born to a stray. That single mutation gave the breed its rippled coat and its unmistakable elfin face.

Everything about the head is oversized and charming. Huge low-set ears, wide cheekbones, big mischievous eyes, and a short muzzle give it a pixie or even alien look. The body is slender and the coat is short, soft, and wavy, sometimes so fine it feels like suede.

The personality is the real draw. Devons are clowns, endlessly playful, clever, and busy, famous for riding around on shoulders and wedging into warm laps. People call them a monkey in a catsuit, and they bond hard to their chosen people.

Because the coat is so thin, Devons love warmth and will seek out the sunniest spot or the nearest neck. They are sociable and food-motivated and stay kittenish for life. The breed is distinct from the Cornish Rex, which carries a different curl gene.

Life with a Devon is rarely dull. It is relentlessly social and busy, prone to following you, perching on shoulders, and stealing the warm spot the second you leave it. It learns tricks, plays fetch, and grows lonely if left alone for long, so a companion or a lively household suits it.

The thin coat needs little brushing but changes the care list in other ways. Devons feel the cold and seek warmth, their large ears need regular cleaning, and the skin can grow oily and want an occasional gentle bath. The breed can carry a muscle condition called spasticity, so health-tested lines matter.

Origin of The Name

Named for Devon, the English county where the founding curly kitten was born, and Rex for its rippled, wavy coat.

A Natural Mutation

The whole breed descends from a single curly-coated kitten named Kirlee, born to a stray in 1960.

Good To Know

Affectionate to the point of clingy, the Devon wants to be with you and seeks warmth. It is active and clever and bores without play.

Coat & Color

A short, fine, wavy coat that can feel like suede, in any color or pattern. Some areas may be only lightly furred.

By the Numbers

Carries a different curl gene than the Cornish Rex. A steady favorite, often the most popular shorthaired breed on CFA’s list.

Living With One

Give it warmth, company, and constant play. The Devon hates being alone and invents mischief if it is under-stimulated.

Health Notes

Clean the big ears regularly and bathe occasionally for oily skin. The breed can carry a muscle disorder, so choose tested lines.

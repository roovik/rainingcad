Australian Cattle Dog

The drover’s right hand, tireless, clever, and faintly bossy.

The Australian Cattle Dog was built to move stubborn cattle across an enormous, brutal country.

Nineteenth-century drovers needed a dog tough enough for punishing heat and long distances. They crossed their herding dogs with the wild dingo and added dashes of collie and Dalmatian. Out came a compact, weatherproof powerhouse.

It works the way a good foreman works. It drives stock from behind, nips at heels, and ducks the kicks. That earned it the nickname heeler, blue or red by the speckle of its coat.

It also holds a record. A Cattle Dog named Bluey lived to twenty-nine, the oldest verified age for any dog. The AKC recognized the breed in 1980.

All that drive needs an outlet. The Cattle Dog is intelligent, intense, and deeply loyal. It is not a dog for a quiet home. A bored heeler will manage your household whether you asked it to or not.

Give it a job and it gives you everything. It bonds hard to one or two people and watches over them with a drover’s seriousness.

Origin of the Name

Named for the job and the country. Also called the blue or red heeler, for its speckled coat and its habit of nipping heels.

The Oldest Dog Ever

A Cattle Dog named Bluey lived twenty-nine years, the longest-lived dog on record.

Good to Know

Bred to work all day in hard country. Needs serious exercise and a job. A bored Cattle Dog herds, nips, and manages the household.

Coats & Colors

A short, dense, weatherproof coat in blue or red speckle, often with patches and a mask.

By the Numbers

Recognized by the AKC in 1980. About five puppies to a litter. A favorite for dog sports and ranch work.

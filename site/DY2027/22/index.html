German Shepherd

Some dogs notice things. German Shepherds notice everything.

The German Shepherd is a young breed with an old purpose. In 1899 a German cavalry officer, Max von Stephanitz, set out to build the ideal working dog. He prized brains over ribbons.

The first dog he registered, Horand, founded the line. Nearly every Shepherd alive traces back to that one choice.

The pasture was only the start. Within a generation the breed had traded herding for the police beat, the battlefield, and the rescue site. It guides the blind. It finds what is hidden. It works the rubble after the worst days.

Fame came early. In 1918 an American soldier found a litter of pups in a bombed kennel in France. One, Rin Tin Tin, became a Hollywood star and made the breed a household name.

The mind is the marvel. A Shepherd reads a situation, weighs it, and decides. It is confident without being reckless and protective without being cruel.

Give it a job and a person to do it for. A bored Shepherd writes its own job description, and you may not like the terms.

Origin of the Name

Plain and literal: the German shepherd dog. After World War I the AKC dropped German for years, and Britain used Alsatian.

Notable German Shepherds

Rin Tin Tin, a Hollywood star from 1918. Buddy, America’s first Seeing Eye dog, guiding Morris Frank in 1928.

Good to Know

Bred to work, so a bored Shepherd invents a job. Needs exercise, training, and company. Sheds hard. Good breeders screen hips and elbows.

Coats & Colors

A dense double coat that sheds year-round. Black and tan, sable, solid black, and bi-color.

By the Numbers

About eight puppies to a litter. Recognized by the AKC in 1908. By most counts the world’s most used police and military dog.

Basset Hound

A nose with legs, and ears that arrive first.

A Basset Hound is mostly nose, ears, and sorrow. It is also mostly joy. It simply hides it under a lot of skin.

The breed began in France. Bas means low in French. Monks and nobles bred short-legged hounds from the old St. Hubert hounds, so hunters on foot could keep up.

A Basset’s nose is second only to the Bloodhound’s. Many people believe the long ears sweep scent toward the nose, and the loose skin holds it there. Every part is built for one job, following a trail.

The Basset came to America in the 1800s, and the AKC recognized it in 1885. Then television and one famous shoe company made it a star.

At home, a Basset is slow, loyal, and stubborn. It will follow its nose past your call, then give you the look that says it did not hear. It loves children, naps, and food, in any order.

Keep it lean, keep the ears clean, and skip the stairs. A Basset needs a gentle leader and an unhurried walk.

Origin of the Name

Bas is French for low. A Basset is a low hound, bred to hunt close to the ground.

Notable Bassets

Flash, Rosco’s sidekick on The Dukes of Hazzard. The Hush Puppies shoe mascot, the best-known Basset of all.

Good to Know

The long back needs support, so skip jumps and stairs. Ears need regular cleaning. Easy to overfeed.

Coats & Colors

A short, smooth coat in tricolor or lemon and white. Loose skin, and ears that reach the floor.

By the Numbers

Forty to sixty-five pounds. Eleven to fifteen inches tall. Recognized by the AKC in 1885.
